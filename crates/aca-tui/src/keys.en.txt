Keys in the aca interface — press ? on any page of the interface for that page's keys; this is every key in detail.
Once inside a session: Ctrl+Q (or tmux prefix + d) brings you back to the interface; the session keeps running.
Ctrl+Q is bound by aca in tmux and only counts in aca's sessions (including windows / panes opened inside them); in an
ordinary tmux session the key still goes to the program inside, so come back with prefix + d. If you bound Ctrl+Q yourself, aca leaves it alone.
The key that opened a page or popup closes it again (as Esc does).

Moving around
  ↑↓ j k    select · g / G top, bottom · J / K next, previous group · digits jump directly (1 then 2 = number 12) · tap to select, tap again to open
  Wheel     over the list switches sessions; over the preview scrolls back through the output (z reads further back)
  h l ← →   out / in a level: h goes from a window / subagent / subsession to the session it hangs off; already at the
            outermost level (a session, a group heading) the cursor goes onto the rail, on the row of where the list is
            (All, or the group viewed). Under 130 columns the screen keeps no rail: h lays it over the left.
            l on a group heading goes into that group (the list shows only its sessions; so does Enter).
            A session that sent out subagents (a +N after its title, or the line atop the preview): l unfolds them
            into rows, l again enters the first; h on a session with them unfolded folds them back first. On a
            session with none, l does nothing.
  Rail      down the left of a screen 130 columns or wider: All, Pinned, every group (subgroups further in), the Trash;
            at the right of each row how many sessions going there lists (by what w is on, or the state picked), and
            how many of them are ◆ ◐ ✕ ●. With the
            cursor on it: ↑↓ j k go to the next place (the list shows it at once, beside it the group's directory and
            pinned notes), g / G first, last; Enter / l / → go in: the cursor is back in the list, on the row it was
            on when that place was left; on the Trash, Enter opens it. On a group's row N, r, d, e, t, Q, ⇧↑ ⇧↓ work
            as on its heading, Tab turns the middle to the group's other page, and o, s open and reply to its
            router (see “A group's router” below); on the All row those two open and reply to the desk; keys that
            act on a session do nothing there. Click a row to go there, click again to go in.
            Esc inside a group goes onto the rail too.
            With a scratch directory set (s in ^S, [scratch] dir in config.toml): the group that works in it (one
            named "scratch" is created if none does) comes right after All, before Pinned, and first in the list;
            with no group's session under the cursor (the rail's All row, a plain tmux session, an empty list) N / n
            start the new session in that group, in its directory. It can be renamed, not deleted.
            A ⌂ leads its name, and stands for it in the names of the groups under it (fun/manga is written ⌂/manga).
            What you type as a group's name, and the command line, keep the whole path.
            A screen under 130 columns: h (or a click on the tag at the left of the top bar) lays the rail over the
            left (the whole width on a phone); Enter / l go in and put it away, h / Esc put it away and go back to
            what the list showed before. There, Esc inside a group goes back to all groups
  Tab       inside a group: its next page, round tasks / sessions / notes (the top row says how many each has —
            for tasks, the tickets not done — the one showing underlined); a group comes back on the page it was
            left on, and on its tasks while it has tickets not done. All groups has one page: Tab does nothing.
            The tasks page: two lines a ticket (its state, title, #number, how long ago; then the one thing that
            matters most now: what its session asks, the line on the ticket, what was said handing it over, the
            result once done). ✓ done  · todo, one being done has its session's own mark. Done ones first, then
            by number; beside it the one under the cursor: what counts as done, who handed it to whom and what
            they said, and below that the session doing it. ↑↓ j k, digits, g / G pick; Enter opens the session
            doing it, s replies to it / answers it; n makes one (its title, then what counts as done), e rewrites
            it; m hands it to one of the group's sessions (which is sent the ticket; "take it back" = to nobody),
            c marks it done, d then y deletes it (for good); h / Esc go out onto the rail.
            After a session's title is the ticket it's doing (#9), or with none the one it finished last (✓ #8);
            after a group's name on the rail, done / all. What waits for what and what comes next is the group's
            router's to decide: aca keeps, shows and passes word. The command line is aca task (aca help task)
            The notes page: two lines a note (★ = pinned, its name = its first line, who wrote it, how long since
            it changed; then how it goes on), pinned ones first, then the most recently changed; beside it the one
            under the cursor in full. ↑↓ j k, digits, g / G pick; e rewrites it, n writes a new one (written at the
            right, its first line is its name; saved as you type, Enter is a newline, Esc finishes, an empty one
            isn't kept, ^G writes it in $EDITOR); p pins / unpins; d then y deletes (into the trash: ^Z / b bring
            it back); h / Esc go out onto the rail. Keys that act on a session do nothing there. Agents read and
            write the same notebook with aca note (see aca help note)
  Space     jump: the number of each session on screen turns into yellow letters, press them to open that one; Space
            again or Esc cancels. Each group has a capital letter of its own while they show, before its name on the
            rail (with no rail: on its heading in all groups; on a rail laid over with h, on that): ⇧ and the letter goes
            into that group, on the page and row it was left on (a group with no session: onto its rail row). A group
            keeps its letter whatever is listed (taken from its name, in the rail's order); adding, deleting or
            reordering groups can change it. A capital no group has only puts the letters away
  a         to all groups, jumping: from inside a group or from the rail, the list becomes all groups (on the row it
            was left on) with the letters showing. a again while they show, or later in all groups, goes back to the
            group and row a was pressed on (onto the rail if the cursor was there). With nowhere to go back to (all
            groups was never left) a is the same as Space
  `         pick a group on the rail: the cursor goes onto the rail (with no rail one is laid over the left), on the
            row of where the list is, with the groups' letters showing (the same letters as ⇧ and a letter after
            Space; no ⇧ here). A letter puts the cursor on that group's row: its sessions in the middle, the exchange
            with its router beside; then that row's keys, ⏎ goes in, o into its router, s says something to it.
            ` again or Esc while the letters show goes back to where ` was pressed; any other key puts the letters
            away and acts as usual (j k move along the rail)
  i         open the next one to look at: ◆ needs you first, then ◐ unread, within a kind the most recently active first
            (the order Ctrl+Alt+N goes in); come back and press i again for the next (ones you've seen with no new
            output since are skipped)
  /         filter; the highlight goes to the match with the most recent activity (open ones first)

Opening, or just looking
  ⏎         open. Not running (■): start it again in place. A Claude router starts fresh; other sessions resume
  E         open with the draft (Shift+Enter works too in desktop kitty): see “Drafts” below
  z         full-screen reading: just look at the output without opening it, scroll back through history (↑↓/jk scroll,
            Space/b page, ^D/^U half page, g/G top/bottom, J/K switch session, Tab shows the diff, c copies (as c on the list), Enter opens it,
            Esc/q/z back)
  x         the diff: of the git repository the session is really working in, with the file list on top; by default
            uncommitted changes (new files included), m switches to including commits (against the fork point from the
            main branch), press again to switch back; n / p next / previous file, e opens the spot at the top of the
            screen in $EDITOR (at that line), y opens lazygit in this repository and rereads after; Tab switches to the
            record, z to the output, the rest as in full-screen reading; refreshes every 2 seconds, read-only (the index
            is never touched)
  Z         session record (like a Langfuse / Phoenix trace): two panes on the desktop, the conversation on the left
            (one block per turn: what you said, what Claude / Codex said last, when · how long · cost at API prices;
            long ones show their start, o shows all), the selected turn's steps on the right (calls, tools and the
            largest context on top; numbers per call, the first sentence of thinking, what it said along the way, tool changes as
            red/green lines and the start of the output). → steps through the right side, o expands / collapses a step
            (full thinking, changes with context and line numbers, the content Read returned, Bash's whole command and
            the first 200 lines of output; ▸ more hidden, ▾ expanded, O the whole turn), ⏎ shows a step full-screen
            (token breakdown, the tool's input), ← / Esc back to the conversation; on a phone the conversation is
            full-screen and ⏎ shows the steps full-screen.
            Code is highlighted by language; added / removed lines in changes get a light green / red background.
            e views the selected item read-only in $EDITOR: tools that read / change a file open that file itself,
            at the line read / changed (vim / nvim with -M, so nothing can be changed or saved); anything else (a
            turn, a call, a command and its output) is written to a Markdown file that's deleted after you close it.
            n / p next / previous turn, J / K switch session, Tab / z output, x diff; a running session keeps growing
            (when you're at the newest).
            A step that started a subagent (Claude's Agent, Codex's spawn_agent) shows its numbers and its last words;
            ⏎ goes in to see its own record in the same two panes, Esc comes back; its cost counts toward the turn that
            started it. Only reads conversation records (Claude; Codex from 0.107, where exec records the commands it
            actually ran and the files it changed); turns copied in at the start of a continuation / fork / subthread
            fold into one "earlier" line and aren't counted; Codex's rolled-back turns are gray and unnumbered, their cost counted
  t         temporary terminal: a shell in the lower half of the interface, in the directory it's really working in;
            all keys go to it, Esc hides it (the shell keeps running; t on the same row brings it back), exit inside
            closes it; carries the session's account directory (CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR / CODEX_HOME)
  T         new window: go in and open a new window running a shell in its tmux session (like prefix c), landing in
            the new window. The new pane is your own shell: without the session's id (aca new inside it isn't made its
            child), the account's config directory kept
  y         open yazi in the directory it's really working in (a stopped session uses the directory it recorded);
            q inside quits back to aca; in a tmux popup it runs in a split next to that pane, zoomed, and switches
            there; q closes that split
  c         copy; the next key says what (the bottom bar lists them): c the last reply · b a reference to the
            session: two lines saying which session it is, the dir it works in, and the commands to read it (aca
            output) and message it (aca msg), for pasting to another agent · i aca id (for commands like aca move <id>)
            · s session id (Claude / Codex's own, for claude --resume) · p path · a all (the dir it really works in,
            the conversation id and the config dir, for pasting outside aca). Missing ones say why; Esc cancels. On a
            phone it goes via OSC 52 into the phone's clipboard
  prefix b  insert a reference (the same key as inside a session): pick a session, its reference goes into the
            highlighted session's input box, no Enter; then back on that row. Esc cancels

  The directory it's really working in: the current directory Claude / Codex track themselves (following cd and
  worktrees); if that's not a repository, the repository its recently changed files are in; when it differs from
  the registered directory, the top of the diff page says so.

Creating, taking over
  N         new with current settings: reuse the selected session's tool, account and directory; change the path if needed (Tab
            completes, with several candidates Tab again cycles, Shift-Tab goes back, / or → takes it; ^W deletes a
            level, ^U clears), ↓ or ^F fuzzy-finds a directory with fzf
            (● has sessions ◇ registered project ↗ zoxide frequent, blank = nearby; Tab next), Enter / double-click
            opens there; → puts it back in the input, keep typing a name = create under it; missing directories are
            created.
            Group: an unchanged path means the selected session's group; a changed one decides by the path (the
            same rules as aca new -g ., see aca help group; the popup says which); tool and
            account follow the selected session; ^T switches to a shell (open a shell, cd and start claude / codex
            yourself), press again to switch back
  n         new: choose a tool and account (digits pick directly), then fill the path. The menu
            lists accounts registered in config.toml (named by account, Claude first then Codex; a harness with no
            account registered is listed by itself), one row each: name, tool, logged in or not, quota (the same row
            wherever accounts are listed), then tools you
            named yourself ([tools.<name>], with their command);
            ⇧↑↓ moves the selected row, including shell. The order is saved in [ui] menu; hide hides entries.
            i in the menu logs the highlighted account in (asking first when it is), a opens the accounts page on it;
            + adds an account: it opens the accounts page, three steps: pick Claude Code or
            Codex first, then the name and the config directory, recorded under [accounts.…] in config.toml; a
            directory that belongs to the other tool (~/.codex as a Claude account) is refused. A new directory gets
            a terminal in the lower half to log in
            (that's aca run TOOL:ACCOUNT -- login: authentication is the tool's own business, aca keeps no credentials)
  ^E        special environments: accounts bound to a cage or a set of environment variables aren't in n; they're
            listed here, one block per environment, its heading saying where it runs, its time zone and proxy;
            ⏎ / digits create with it, i logs in, a the accounts page. ^E in n switches here too, and then Esc goes
            back to n (aca help config)
  ^N        new group: name it first (“parent/name” works, spaces become -), then Enter goes straight to fzf to pick a
            directory (the first line is the default directory, [new_group] default_path in config.toml, ~/workspace
            if unset); the new session opens in this group, and the directory becomes the group's default path
  f         fork: open another session carrying the conversation, with its group, account and tool, as a child of the
            original; name it in the popup first (prefilled “<title> (fork)”, Enter takes it unlocked so Claude can
            still rename it; a changed name is locked, ^U clears it); it starts right away and the highlight follows.
            Only for registered Claude / Codex sessions, and only once there's a conversation
  H         hand off: open another session to carry on its work. Pick the new session's tool / account first (the n
            menu, without shell); it's created in its directory and group, and the handoff note is sent over: its
            conversation record, an extracted text version (what both sides said, subagent reports, the files it
            changed, in ~/.local/state/aca/ui/handoff/), the directory, the conversation id, and its draft; the new
            session reads it all and carries on, asking you first only if the original ended with a question its draft
            doesn't answer. The original is left alone; while it's still running the popup says both would change this
            directory at once. If it can't be sent it stays in the new session's draft (s replies with it)
  X         carry on under another account (for when one ran out of quota mid-task): no new session — it restarts in
            place on another account of the same kind. A Claude router starts a new shift. Other sessions resume
            with their record copied to the new account. The row, tmux session, title and draft stay. The box shows the
            current account and its quota on top, then the accounts it can move to: logged in or not, and quota
            (5h / 7d used; amber past 70%, red past 90%, as on the billing page). ⏎ or a digit switches; a logged-out
            one can't be picked: i logs it in right here (then X again), a opens the accounts page, + adds one. If it's
            running or needs you the box says switching interrupts the turn. The new account's cache is cold:
            the first turn writes the whole context again. A session stopped by quota or login shows the error and
            "X switch account" under its row. Same as aca restart <id> --as ACCOUNT
  A         adopt: register an ordinary tmux session (not started by aca: by hand, by agent-deck) as aca's, without
            stopping it. A confirmation shows which agent, which account, which conversation it recognized and what the
            session will be renamed to; only y adopts. Same as aca adopt (what it recognizes and what to watch out for
            afterwards: aca help adopt). Once adopted it can be renamed, moved, restarted, forked

On sessions
  D         stop (asks first, y): the agent exits, its pane stays with the last screen (other windows inside are unaffected), the
            registration stays, the list shows ■; ⏎ starts it again in place (a Claude router starts fresh; Codex
            only has a conversation id once it has talked; one that never did starts fresh).
            Sessions gone from tmux entirely (after a reboot) and restored archived ones are listed the same way, ⏎
            recreates them; one whose last turn ended in an error shows ✕.
            One aca stopped for idling shows ☾ and "auto-stopped" on its row; ⏎ starts it the same way. For ordinary sessions, put
            [idle] stop_after = "3h" in config.toml and while the interface is open, every 5 minutes it stops the
            sessions idle that long; never one mid-turn, attached, with background tasks, with unsent text in its
            input box or with something scheduled not yet due (CronCreate, /loop, aca msg --in) (see aca help stop).
            [idle] remove_after = "30d": ones stopped that long are deleted into the trash like d while the
            interface is open, as one entry that ^Z / b brings back; not pinned ones or ones with a draft
            D on a window / pane opened inside a session, or on an ordinary tmux session: stops only that pane (the
            program stops, the pane stays, shows ■), ⏎ starts its original command again in place
  R         restart in place (a Claude router starts fresh; other sessions resume by default): asks first if it's running / needs you
  r         rename (in a popup with the title prefilled; the tmux session name follows, and it stops following the
            agent's own title).
            On a group (its heading / its rail row) r edits the group: rename first (“parent/name” = move under another
            group; subgroups and sessions follow, workspaces too), then Enter edits the default directory (prefilled;
            Tab completes; empty = forget it; N / t on the group use it, aca new -g . in this directory goes into this
            group; a directory is the default of one group at most). Same as aca group rename / dir
  m         move to another group: only the group changes, the session keeps running in its directory. Pick with fzf;
            each row shows that group's directory (its default, or where its sessions are), the one in the same
            directory first, then list order; type a name that doesn't exist and press Enter = create that group and move it in.
            The new group goes under the session's group and works in a directory of that name under that group's
            (made if missing; none when that group has no directory); this is written above the list before you press
            Enter, and Tab changes the directory first. A name with a / in it is the whole path from the top (/name = at the top)
  ⇧↑ ⇧↓     move up / down within the group; on a group it moves the group
  d         delete the selected session; only y deletes, any other key cancels. A registered session goes to the trash
            (tmux session closed, registration kept) and is really deleted a day later; until then ^Z / b bring it
            back; windows opened inside close with it (the confirmation says how many). Ordinary tmux sessions / panes
            are killed directly, no undo; if an ordinary tmux session has other windows, only the first pane closes and
            the rest move up; d on the last one closes the whole session.
            Before closing, the processes inside are cleaned up (SIGHUP + SIGTERM, SIGKILL after two seconds).
            On a group (its heading / its rail row / an empty group) d deletes the group: the confirmation says how many sessions
            and subgroups go with it, y deletes. Subgroups and the sessions inside (archived ones too) go to the trash
            as one entry: ^Z / b bring the whole group back, with its order, default directory, workspace and notes
  b         trash: what d deleted stays a day, one row per delete (a deleted group with its sessions is one row), newest
            first. ⏎ brings it back (sessions come back stopped, ⏎ again starts it), d then y deletes it
            for good now. A note deleted on its own (d on the notes page, aca note rm) is a row too (✎ name): ⏎ puts
            it back in its group (a group deleted meanwhile comes back with it).
            Selects as in the list (v range, V range unselect, Space one row, Esc unselects all, then
            closes), and ⏎ / d act on the selected rows together. Entries older than a day are deleted when an
            interface opens, refreshes or exits
  ^Z        undo the last step (step by step, up to 10, cleared on exit): d delete (what was open starts again) / D stop
            (starts again) / m move / r rename / M attach-detach MCP / p pin / u seen-unread /
            Q send to workspace / e draft / r on a group / a batch done with v (all in one step); R restart, s reply,
            f fork, A adopt aren't undone
  M         MCP: lists this session's MCPs, [ ] editable, · read-only. A Claude session can attach the ones from
            config.toml ([mcps.<name>], every key but description goes into .mcp.json as-is) to the project (Space
            toggles, Enter applies; written to .mcp.json in its registered directory, takes effect after a restart).
            When attaching, .mcp.json goes into this repository's local .git/info/exclude (not committed); detaching
            all deletes the empty .mcp.json; one already in the repository is left alone. Global ones (in the account
            directory's .claude.json) and Codex's (CODEX_HOME/config.toml) are listed, not changed. With the gateway
            plugin on, the top rows are the servers this session sees through the gateway: Space switches one off or
            on for this session only, Enter applies at once (Claude picks it up now, Codex after a restart); an
            [mcps.<name>] served by the gateway isn't offered again under project
  p         pin / unpin. Pinned ones sit in their own block at the top (earliest pinned first), listed even after a
            day without activity
  u         seen / unread: on ◐ marks it seen, on ○ marks it unread (◐ until you open it); opening it counts as
            seen too (passing through the one you just left on the way into another, so ^` gets you back, does not),
            and finishing another turn brings back ◐
  v / V     select several and act on them together (as in yazi): v starts a range at the highlighted row, ↑↓ j k /
            digits / J K / a tap stretch it, and Esc ends it; V stretches a range the same way that unselects.
            v or V in the middle of a range drops the range not kept yet and starts a new one at the highlighted row.
            Space selects / unselects one and moves down (on a group heading: the sessions listed in it; in a range
            too, which then stretches). Not in a range, Esc unselects all.
            Selected rows turn green with a green mark in front, and the bottom bar says how many and what you can
            press: d delete / D stop / R restart (asks first, listing which ones and how many are running and would
            be interrupted; restart doesn't ask when none is running) · m move · u read / unread · p pin / unpin ·
            Q+key send to a workspace. Done = no longer selected; deleted together = one trash entry, and ^Z undoes
            the whole batch in one step. Only registered sessions (not windows, subagents,
            or ordinary tmux sessions); filtered-out ones stay selected. With just one selected
            it's the same as pressing the key on it

  Windows opened inside a session / split panes: d closes only that pane, D stops only that pane; R / r / m don't apply.
  Ordinary tmux sessions (unregistered): you can open, look, D / d; renaming, restarting, forking, moving need A first.

Drafts (the next thing to say to this session)
  e         write: in the upper half of the preview (in a popup when the preview is off); saved as you type, Enter
            is a newline, Esc finishes, ^G writes it in $EDITOR (^G works straight from the list too). The list
            shows ✎. It's only kept; opening the session with Enter leaves it alone.
            On a group (its heading / its rail row) e writes a new note in the group's notebook instead (see the
            notes page under Tab above): ✎ after the group name when it has notes, the pinned ones named under its
            directory in the preview (★ name); never filled into or sent to a session, they follow the group when
            it's renamed and go to the trash with it
  E         open with the draft: filled into its input box, not sent, then deleted, like Claude Code's stash;
            when it needs you (◆), is stopped, or isn't Claude / Codex, it doesn't open and says why
  s         reply: say something to it without going in. Written where e writes, under 「↩ Reply」, its draft already
            in it if it has one; ⏎ sends it (pasted into its input box and Enter, you stay in the list; a running one
            queues it), ^J / Shift+⏎ is a newline, Esc keeps it as the draft unsent, ^G writes it in $EDITOR. Saved to
            the draft as you type, deleted once sent. ⏎ looks at its state again: if it's waiting for you now, nothing
            is sent and the text stays in its draft. On the row of a window / split pane opened inside a session with a
            built-in agent in it (an agent team's teammate) it goes into that pane. On a stopped Claude router's row, s
            wakes it with your message. For other stopped sessions or agents outside the built-in set, it says why you can't reply.
            On one waiting for you (◆) it answers instead: its question is listed, a single choice takes the option's
            number, a permission prompt y to allow (this once) / n to deny, a startup prompt the option's number;
            anything else (several questions, multiple choice, writing your own answer) takes ⏎ to go in and answer.
            Esc / q / s close it, any other key just closes it too, nothing answered; it closes by itself when the
            question changes

A group's router (the session that hands the group's work to the right sessions for you)
  @         a group has at most one router, one of its own sessions. An @ in the color of the router's state leads
            the group's name on the rail, its heading in all groups, and the router's own row. With the cursor on the
            group's row (on the rail / its heading) the column beside is the exchange with it: ❯ what you said ·
            ● what it answered · @ → name what it handed a session, a session it started · @ ← group name a session
            it pulled over · name ❯ what another session sent it; “not delivered” on what hasn't arrived, bold for what
            came since you last looked, and the bottom line (❯) is where a reply to it goes. Only the latest is
            listed. A gray @ means stopped. A Claude router stops after [router] idle (one hour by default). Send with s
            to wake it, or start it with o or with ⏎ on its own row. Every start opens a fresh conversation.
            The first message is aca's handoff with tickets, recent exchanges and pending messages.
            Its role file sets the rules. It gets aca's dispatch tools and a question tool, no Bash, and skips
            the project's CLAUDE.md and memory.
            A Codex router gets its rules in its opening message, stays up when idle, holds incoming messages
            while stopped, and resumes the previous conversation when you start it.
            When the router passes on your request, aca carries your words to the worker. Ask it to watch a ticket
            for updates. Unwatched results go straight into the exchange. Completion wakes the router only for a
            watched ticket or when the group has another ticket to assign
  o         into the router of the group the row is in (one that isn't running is started first): on a group's row
            that group's, on a session's row the one of the group the session is in, the desk for the scratch group
            and its sessions; back from it with Ctrl+Q the cursor is still on that row (an interface opened inside
            tmux switches over to it and closes, as ⏎ does).
            With no router yet, the new-session box opens in the group's directory. Use ↑ to choose a Claude or
            Codex tool and account, then ⏎ to create and open “<group> router”. Naming an existing session, or nobody:
            aca group router (aca help group).
            Plain tmux sessions and ones in no group have none. Another group's router: ` and its letter, then o
  s         on a group's row: say something to its router, written on the bottom line of that column, with the keys
            of s on a session's row (⏎ sends, Esc keeps it as its draft); when it needs you (◆) it's the answer box,
            where ⏎ goes into its conversation. Send to a stopped Claude router as usual. For a stopped Codex router, press o to start it
  Desk      the router of the scratch group (see “Rail” above) is the desk: hand it what you don't know whom to give

As a switcher (a tmux popup)
  aca tmux install sets it up: Ctrl+\ without the prefix opens it, on the right (56 columns) on a wide enough window,
  full screen otherwise (a phone) — tmux can't open a popup wider than the window; prefix + a always opens it full
  screen; the status bar gets how many to look at (" ◆ 2  ◐ 3": ◆ needs you, ◐ unread, same symbols as the list). It
  lives in aca's own tmux file (~/.config/aca/tmux.conf); your tmux.conf only gains one source-file line, and aca tmux
  uninstall removes it. To bind it yourself, copy from that file
  Ctrl+Alt+N  pressed inside a session (no list): goes to the next one to look at, ◆ before ◐; again for the one
            after (aca help next). aca binds it, the way Ctrl+Q is; another key: aca tmux key next <key>
  prefix b  tmux's prefix key (Ctrl+B by default), then b, inside a session: pops up the list on the right; ⏎ puts
            the picked session's reference (the two lines c b copies) into the input box, no Enter (aca ui --ref). aca
            binds it too; another key: aca tmux key ref <key> (prefix b means b after the prefix key)
  --client  Enter switches only the client that pressed the key (phone and desktop each their own), and it opens with
            that client's current session highlighted
  --follow  follow: wherever the highlight goes, the side switches to that session and the popup stays open (it's
            attached to the client). It switches once the highlight settles (not while sliding past with an arrow
            held), not while typing a filter, and not on a group or a session that isn't running.
            ⏎ stays · Esc goes back to the pane you opened it from · Ctrl+\ or q q closes, leaving you on the one
            now showing beside it.
            Passing by isn't seeing: an unread one (◐) stays ◐ after you pass it; only ⏎ staying or closing
            on it counts as seen.
            No preview is drawn (the real thing is right beside it); L still switches it, just this once, without
            touching the layouts phone and desktop remember
  Ctrl+\    pressed again inside the popup = close it (also for popups that don't follow)
  ^`        back to the previous session (same as ^` in tmux)

Reading the list
  Order     by group, each under a line with its name and how many sessions it has (on the desktop the number is at the
            right, under the times; on a narrow screen ▾ group  count); group order and order within a group come from the registry (⇧↑ ⇧↓
            move them); ungrouped sessions come after the last group, ordinary tmux sessions (not created by aca) at
            the bottom; changes in state or activity don't move anything.
            One row per session (numbered on the desktop; directory and branch are in the preview header), with an
            extra row under one that needs you showing what it asks.
            Windows opened / panes split inside a session hang under it (├ #2 / └ #2.1, tmux's numbering), each with
            its own state and preview; ⏎ lands in that pane.
            Child sessions (created with aca new inside a session, given --parent, or forked) hang under their parent
            when in the same group (├ title), and a child's own children one level further in (the indent stops after
            4 levels; deeper ones are drawn side by side); filtering keeps every level above a child that stays;
            pinning a session takes everything under it up; deleting a parent doesn't take its children, the
            confirmation says so
  Subagents The ones Claude Code / Codex send out themselves (Claude's Agent tool, Codex's spawn_agent). They run
            inside the session's process and have no pane, so they take no row by default: the +N after a session's
            title is how many are still running. When the main conversation has stopped but background subagents
            are still running, the session counts as ●, not as unread. l unfolds them: one row each, right
            under the session and above its windows (├ ● type · description, dimmed, not numbered; ✓ finished
            ✕ failed ■ stopped, one started by another subagent carries ↳), the last column is how long it ran.
            Claude's default type (general-purpose) is left out, only the description shows; Codex's read
            "name · task name" (older versions: name · role · the first line of what it was told).
            Listed are the ones still running plus the ones started in the last turn; on an idle session finished
            ones stay at most half an hour, older ones are in Z. With the highlight on one: the preview is a few
            lines written from its record — while it runs, its task and one line per step, newest at the bottom;
            once it has reported, its report comes first and is read from the top (newer Codex versions encrypt
            what a child is told, so only the task name can be shown). ⏎ / Z opens the session's record at that
            subagent, Esc returns to the list (← goes up a level, to whoever started it); it isn't a session, so
            rename, delete, drafts and the like don't apply. Only the record files are read, the same ones Z
            shows; when the path the hooks reported can't be used (no aca hooks, or Claude moved the record on
            entering / leaving a worktree) the record is found through the process; a Codex session without
            hooks can't be matched to its conversation and shows none. Teammates split off by Claude's agent
            teams are real panes and are listed as panes
  State     ● running (the last column is how long)  ◆ needs you (a choice / confirmation)  ◐ unread (finished, not seen yet)
            ○ idle  ✕ error  ■ stopped (the agent exited / stopped with D / gone from tmux)  ☾ auto-stopped (by aca, for idling)
            The top bar counts what the list lists (the group, w's choice, a state picked, a search).
            ✎ has a draft  ★ pinned  ⇄ open elsewhere too
            ◷ 08:57 after a title: something scheduled not yet due, with when the first one runs and +N for more;
            the preview lists them. Claude's CronCreate and /loop's next wakeup live only in its process: stopping
            or restarting drops them. A scheduled inbox message (aca msg --in) keeps an ordinary session up until it is due.
            A Claude router can stop and wake at delivery
            Registered sessions: what the screen shows (asking, running) wins, what it can't show (unread,
            error) comes from the hooks; other sessions go by the screen only.
            Open = ● ◆ ◐ ○ ✕ (everything but stopped)
  Account   in gray, the ▪ before it in the tool's color: orange = Claude Code, blue = Codex; registered sessions
            show the registered account, hand-started ones (a shell session / claude / codex in an ordinary tmux
            session) are matched to account names in config.toml by their process's config directory, and the
            directory follows cd
  Time      last column: now / 40s / 12m / 2h / 3d. A registered Claude / Codex that isn't running counts from what its
            hooks reported (the turn finished, it started asking, aca started it): redrawing while idle (settings changed,
            the window resized) isn't activity
  Preview   state, how long ago, uncommitted changes (files +added −removed, nothing when clean), the directory
            it's really working in, the branch; then the draft if there is one

Where things are kept
  Which to list (w), layout (one each for desktop and phone), the highlighted session: still there next time (not
  restored when opened with a keyword). It always opens on all groups.
  All under ~/.local/state/aca/ui/: view, pinned, read, spaces (workspaces), notes; shared by phone and desktop.
  Colors: latte (light) by default, change with the theme in ^S or aca theme use; ACA_THEME=<theme> (dark / light are
  mocha / latte) overrides it.
  Language: English by default; u in ^S settings flips between zh / en on the spot (saved as [ui] lang in config.toml); ACA_LANG=zh works too.
